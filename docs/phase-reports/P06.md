# P06 — Military Finance / Armed Organization

Date: 2026-09-12
Status: complete. No P07 work started.

## Scope

Give the model a garrison and an armed band, and let the plan's M4 and M5 loops close: a county
pays and feeds its soldiers out of the same treasury and granary that relief and taxation use;
unpaid, unfed soldiers desert; deserters go home, to the hills, or to an unorganized pool;
bands form from that pool or from hungry households, live off the land, move when the garrison
can wear them down, and are suppressed, disbanded, split or merged by declared rules. Then run the
sweep the phase asks for and report the measurements — without claiming what they mean
historically.

### Acceptance criteria

```text
GovernmentMilitaryUnit (strength · food · pay due/received · arrears · morale · cohesion) ·
ArmedBand (size · food · arms · mobility · cohesion · network · territorial access) ·
military finance: pay from the treasury, rations from granary and market, morale and cohesion
following this month's shortfalls · desertion as a declared rate with declared destinations ·
armed-group recruitment from a shared pool: the garrison from all adults, a band from the
distress-eligible, in that order · band formation from unorganized deserters or a distress levy ·
band action: raid (grain and war material), rations, movement toward food · violence:
suppression, dissolution, split, merge · metrics: number of bands, largest-band share, band size
distribution, desertion rate · an experiment sweeping the claim, the pay share and the climate ·
tests for people, food, war material, suppression, the recruit pool and the unorganized pool
```

### Non-goals (explicitly not built)

No tactics, no battles, no orders of battle, no named leaders or named bands; no siege, fortification
or terrain in the military graph; no migration of households (P03 records eligibility only, and
still nobody moves); no central government, no inter-county military movement orders; no
calibration; no LLM. Nothing in this phase reads the historical record: every rate is grade `S`.

## Contract decisions

1. **Every rule is arithmetic over measured quantities.** Pay due is strength × a monthly rate;
   the desertion rate is the base plus weighted *current* pay shortfall, *current* ration
   shortfall and fallen morale, capped; suppression reach is garrison strength × effectiveness ×
   (1 − local support) × (1 − arms mitigation); a band takes grain up to a multiple of its own
   monthly need; a band moves when it is hungry or when the local garrison could remove a declared
   share of its people. There is no tactics, no map reading, and no rule that decides who wins.
2. **A band is created empty.** Formations, splits and merges all pass through a logged inflow
   (`adopt`, `gain_members`), because the first version of the split path created a splinter whose
   members were never on its books — the invariant test caught it, as it caught four other defects
   (duplicate event types for raids and internal stores, dissolution handing people home after
   zeroing the band, a levy that was taken and quietly handed back, and an arms mitigation that
   reached the log but not the losses).
3. **Two recruit pools over the same households.** The garrison levies from every adult at its
   node — conscription is an obligation in this model — and a band levies only from the
   distress-eligible ones, because band recruitment is a response to hunger. Both are checked
   against cohort losses tick by tick.
4. **The unorganized pool is an account, recorded every tick.** Deserters waiting to be organized
   are neither home nor enlisted; the phase records the pool whether or not it holds anyone, so
   the identity *routed in − formed out = held* is exact and testable.
5. **Bands need a seat at the table or they starve.** A band's granary loss is the county's gain
   in reverse: grain taken from cohorts, elites and the granary is matched to the shi on both
   sides, per tick, and arms enter the bands only by converting looted property at a declared
   rate — everything else that moves arms is an internal transfer between bands.
6. **Military finance is opt-in.** `with_military` defaults to false, so the P03–P05 runs and
   their pinned digests stay what they were.
7. **The county's monthly record carries the military decomposition.** This re-pinned the P05
   fiscal digest for instrumentation only. Verified by row-level diff of the P05 and P06 logs:
   480 of 28,764 events changed, each gaining exactly `military_pay_tael` and `military_grain_shi`
   (both zero without a garrison), event count unchanged, and no measured fiscal quantity moved.
   The reason is recorded in the test file next to the digest.

What the surface shows, in the model's own terms:

- **The claim size binds; the pay share mostly does not.** A claim of 100 per node is held at
  target (500 troops) in every scenario; claims of 200–450 all settle near 800 troops in total,
  whichever pay share is chosen. Paying more does not buy a larger garrison once the claim exceeds
  what the levy and the desertion floor allow — it buys lower arrears (claim 300: 39,759 → 18,828
  tael from pay 0.2 to 0.8) and a slightly lower desertion rate at the small claim (0.092 → 0.078).
- **Arrears accumulate without limit.** Every scenario ends with arrears on the books; the monthly
  claim exceeds what a county of this size can raise at this nominal pressure, so the stock is
  never cleared. This is a measurement about the toy county's fiscal scale as much as about the
  rule.
- **The bands respond most to the climate, not to the pay share.** Cohort grain taken by raiders
  ranges from 8,808 shi (calm, small claim) to 206,736 shi (shock, large claim); band strength from
  341 to 4,473; 27–46 bands form and 46–70 dissolve over the 240 months, so the armed sector is a
  churn of formation and destruction rather than a standing force.
- **The largest-band share ranges 0.25–0.78** and reaches 1.0 at some point in the small-claim runs.
  The plan's consolidation observable therefore *exists* in the output; whether any of it is
  consolidation rather than a moment when only one band happened to be alive is exactly the kind of
  question the metric cannot answer by itself.
- **The granary is never raided in these runs** (0 shi in all 16 scenarios) while cohorts and
  elites are: relief keeps the granary near empty, so a band finds nothing there. That is a
  consequence of the P05 relief rule, and it is recorded rather than explained away.
- **Splits and merges never fire in these scenarios** (0 in all 16), though both rules exist and are
  exercised directly by unit tests. Bands are fed often enough to stay cohesive, and two cohesive
  bands rarely survive in one node long enough to meet. The phase's honest position: the rules are
  implemented and tested, the default surface does not reach them, and forcing the region (a hungry,
  large, low-cohesion band) is a scenario question for P10.

The six required invariant categories, each against a real 60-tick military run:

- **people** — cohorts, garrisons, bands and the unorganized pool sum to minus the logged
  dispersals, exactly; every deserter who walks away is a logged outflow.
- **food** — per tick, what the bands gained in raids equals what cohorts, elites and the granary
  lost, to within floating-point tolerance.
- **war material** — arms gained by raiding equal seized property × the declared conversion rate,
  and the event types that move band arms are a closed set (raid, internal transfer, dissolution).
- **suppression** — the members a band reports losing to suppression equal the suppression claims
  against it.
- **recruit pool** — cohort losses to garrisons equal garrison gains, and cohort losses to bands
  equal band gains, tick by tick; the two recruiters draw from the same cohorts in the same tick.
- **unorganized pool** — routed in minus formed out equals what is still held, and the held pool
  never reaches the formation size.

Reproducibility: two runs of the same military scenario with the same config produce the same
simulation digest, and the phase's own run is covered by the pinned regression seed (the P05 fiscal
pin was re-pinned for instrumentation, with the row-level diff recorded).

## Open items for P07 and later

- **No migration.** P03 records who *could* leave and nothing leaves; bands and armies therefore
  compete for labour with a population that cannot walk away from either.
- **Morale is nearly saturated.** With arrears entrenched in every toy scenario the morale channel
  contributes a near-constant; the pay lever shows up in arrears and in desertion only weakly. A
  fiscal base large enough to clear a month's pay would be needed to see morale move in earnest —
  P08's sourced pay rates and P09's calibration are the places for that.
- **Splits and merges are untriggered by the default surface** (see above).
- **No labour pulled out of agriculture.** Levies take adults, so they reduce consumption need, but
  no production effect: the harvest rule does not read adults. That link belongs with P07.
- **Suppression has no political cost.** A garrison that suppresses its own population raises
  nobody's resentment, loses no local information and pays no legitimacy price; the plan's M5
  expects the opposite, and it is not modelled yet.
- **Raids have no defensive response.** Elites do not organize defence, households do not flee the
  node, and the trade graph is not disrupted by violence — `TradeDisruption` exists and P06 does
  not drive it.
- **Band identity is a counter.** `band-0007` is a sequence number: nothing carries over when a band
  dissolves and reforms, and cohesion, network and arms start from the fixture each time.
