# P12 — Decision-Policy Robustness

Date: 2026-09-14
Status: complete, with the runtime arm refused by the P11 gate. No P13 work started.

## Scope

Hold the world fixed and change only the rule for deciding. Four arms — a declared threshold policy, a
bounded utility rule, a seeded random control and the runtime model — run the same configuration, the
same parameter values and the same random numbers, and three named mechanisms are read off every run
so the answers are about *what the model does*, not about how a run looked. The scope document is
`docs/exec-plans/P12.md`; the generated matrix is `docs/experiments/policy-robustness.md`.

## The policy-robustness matrix

A cell is the share of that arm's replicates in which the mechanism is present.

| mechanism | rule | utility | random | ustc | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fiscal-extraction-inversion | 0.00 | **1.00** | 0.33 | — | **policy-dependent** (utility) |
| fiscal-military-ratchet | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | — | **absent** |
| armed-band-consolidation | 0.83 | 0.83 | 0.83 | — | **robust** (all three) |

Verdict rules, declared before the runs: a policy *shows* a mechanism when at least half its
replicates have it; *robust* at three or more arms, *mixed* at two, *model-dependent* when the runtime
arm is the only one that shows it, *policy-dependent* when a single declared policy is, *absent* when
none is. The verdicts cover the arms that ran.

## The five questions

### 1. Does Fiscal Extraction Inversion survive a change of policy? No — it is policy-dependent

Present in **6 of 6** utility replicates (median strength 0.18), in **2 of 6** random replicates, and
in **none** of the rule arm's. The inversion — assessment pressure and collection effort rising while
receipts over the quota fall — is therefore not a property of the model's structure at these settings.
It appears under the one policy that reacts to distress by pressing harder, and it does not appear
under a policy that presses by rule or at random.

This is the phase's clearest mechanism answer, and it is worth stating carefully: the mechanism is
*policy-dependent*, not *model-dependent*. No model ran, so nothing here says an LLM-backed policy
would or would not show it.

### 2. Does the Fiscal-Military Ratchet exist? No — absent in every arm

The ratchet was declared as *the arrears stock never falls*: strictly rising pay arrears with no month
of decline. It appears in **none** of the eighteen arm-replicates. In every run the stock declines at
least once — small declines, but declines — so the model's pay arrears accumulate *mostly* rather than
*monotonically*, and the strong form of the ratchet is falsified here.

The reading still reports a large stock at the end of the run (the level comparison below shows the
reference arm's median end-arrears at 23,885 tael against a total assessment of the same order), so
"arrears are structural" survives while "arrears never clear" does not. That distinction is exactly
what the phase's falsifier field exists for.

### 3. Does the crisis tipping region move? Yes, and it moves with the policy

Grid cells where any replicate breaks down (fiscal pressure × climate severity):

| policy | cells with breakdown | same cells as the reference | cells |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rule` | 7 | — | 0.01×0.3, 0.01×0.9, 0.02×0.3, 0.02×0.9, 0.04×0.3, 0.04×0.6, 0.04×0.9 |
| `utility` | 5 | no | 0.01×0.9, 0.02×0.9, 0.04×0.3, 0.04×0.6, 0.04×0.9 |
| `random` | 6 | no | 0.01×0.3, 0.01×0.9, 0.02×0.3, 0.02×0.9, 0.04×0.3, 0.04×0.9 |

The region is not a property of the world alone. The utility arm's region is smaller and shifted
towards high climate severity: it breaks down at 0.9 severity at every pressure but not at the low
severity where the other two do, and its crossed-line counts are lower everywhere (mean crossed 4.5
at 0.01×0.3 against 5.5 for rule and random). A policy that reacts to the readings holds the line
count down in the cells where the others cross.

The grid is two replicates per cell, so a cell's breakdown share is 0, 0.5 or 1 — this is where the
region is, not how likely it is.

### 4. Does armed-band consolidation depend on the model? No — it is robust across policies

Consolidation (fewer bands, with the largest holding a rising share) is present in **5 of 6**
replicates in *every* arm, at nearly the same strength (median 0.118 rule, 0.146 utility, 0.123
random). It is the one mechanism the decision policy does not disturb.

Since no model-backed arm ran, the phase cannot say whether an LLM policy would disturb it. What it
can say is stronger than that in one direction: consolidation is not an artifact of *how* the
institutions decide, because three quite different decision rules all produce it.

### 5. Levels or mechanisms? One mechanism changes; several levels move

Paired differences against the rule arm, on the same replicates (only resolved directions listed):

| metric | arm | rule median | arm median | paired difference (95 % interval) | direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| receipts over quota (total) | `utility` | 0.200 | 0.281 | +0.078 (0.037 … 0.101) | up |
| military pay arrears, end (tael) | `utility` | 23,885 | 17,712 | −3,109 (−9,024 … −179) | down |
| largest band share (max) | `utility` | 0.383 | 0.424 | +0.023 (0.005 … 0.052) | up |
| largest band share (max) | `random` | 0.383 | 0.389 | +0.006 (0.001 … 0.012) | up |

Reading the two tables together: the utility policy *realises more of the assessed quota, runs a
smaller military arrears stock and concentrates armed force more* than the rule arm — the same world
with a different decision rule. The random arm differs from the rule arm in one level only
(concentration), which is what a control should look like.

So the answer is both, and the distinction is the phase's product:

- **what changes** — extraction inversion appears under one policy and not the others (a mechanism
  change), and the tipping region moves (a mechanism-shaped change);
- **how much changes** — receipts, arrears and band concentration all move in level while the
  consolidation mechanism stays put;
- **what does not change** — armed-band consolidation, in all three arms and at three decision rules.

## What this phase found (and fixed)

1. **A verdict rule that conflated two different claims.** The first implementation called any
   mechanism shown by exactly one arm "model-dependent". That is wrong: a mechanism a single *declared
   rule* shows is *policy-dependent*, and only the runtime arm being the sole shower makes it
   model-dependent. The rule now distinguishes them, and the integration tests pin both cases —
   including the model-dependent case, which is tested with a synthetic runtime arm precisely because
   the real one is refused.
2. **The utility policy was missing from the smoke runner**, so the second arm of the comparison could
   not even be built. Found immediately by the first P12 run; added.
3. **A test wrote the phase's report into `docs/`.** `run_p12` had grown a `report_dir` parameter that
   was never threaded through to the writer, so the integration test overwrote the real report with a
   1-replicate version. Fixed, and the real report was regenerated from its artifact — which is the
   phase's own reproducibility claim, exercised by accident and then on purpose.
4. **The ratchet reading needed its falsifier to be real.** Declaring the ratchet as "arrears
   accumulate" would have found it in every arm and said nothing; declaring it as "no month ever
   clears" makes it falsifiable, and it is falsified. The distinction between *structural arrears*
   (which all arms show) and *a ratchet* (which none does) is the phase's second real finding.

  imputing anything;

  cannot drift from the data it summarises;
- the runtime arm is refused with its reason recorded.

## Open items for P13 and later

- **The runtime arm remains unrun.** Confirm the model id, run `uv run pytest -m live_ustc` to record
  the first fixtures, then `run_p12` with the gate open: the matrix has a fourth column waiting, and
  the model-dependent test path is already exercised synthetically.
- **The ratchet is falsified in its strong form and survives in its weak form.** P13 should name the
  mechanism card accordingly — *structural arrears* with an occasional clearing month — rather than
  carrying the ratchet metaphor into the card collection.
- **Extraction inversion is policy-dependent at these settings**, which makes it a candidate for a
  card about *policy response*, not about the crisis: it is what pressing harder produces.
- **Two replicates per grid cell** is enough to place the region and not enough to compare cells; the
  movement is a statement about which cells cross, not about shares.
- **Six replicates per arm** gave resolved directions on three of nine levels for the utility arm and
  one for the random arm. More replicates would sharpen the level comparison; the mechanism verdicts
  are already unambiguous at this size.
- **Only three mechanisms were looked for.** P13 will want more cards, and each new one needs its own
  falsifier before it is measured.
